# DISCiPLIN — Sovereign Standards Log

**Date:** 2026-07-28 (reconciled)
**Scope:** QA audit of `disciplin-ai/disciplin-empire` against the platform's own Constitution, plus a directional comparison against publicly documented governance principles from IMMAF and Abu Dhabi's sports-data governance program.

## Provenance note

This log was originally written against a narrower Coach-Gate implementation (`coach_athlete_links`/`correction_directives`, branch `claude/disciplin-platform-audit-tevxl7`) built without visibility into a more complete, independently-developed implementation that already existed locally (`preserve/codex-current-ui-2026-07-28`). That branch is now the integration base — its authority model (`lib/authority/state.ts`), coach relationship system (`coach_relationships`, `coach_invitations`, `mission_versions`, `mission_submissions`, `current_coach_missions`), workflow contracts, and 17-file test suite supersede the narrower schema this log originally described. The findings below have been re-verified against the reconciled source of truth; claims that no longer hold have been corrected rather than left standing.

## Methodology

Two independent read-only audits were run against the repository: one over the backend (`/api/sensei*`, `/lib/disciplin/*`, `/lib/supabase/sql/*`) focused on whether AI output ever becomes an enforced correction without a human coach; one over the frontend (`/app`, `/components`) focused on the "one question per screen" rule and fabricated-evidence/marketing-fluff prohibitions. Findings were verified directly against source files before any fix was written, then re-verified a second time against the reconciled branch. Fixes were checked with `tsc --noEmit`, `next build`, and `eslint` against a placeholder environment (no live Supabase/OpenAI credentials were available in this environment, so no runtime/browser verification was possible — this is a static and build-time audit, not a live QA pass).

## Why finding #4 was downgraded on inspection

The initial backend audit flagged the Proof Judge as "an AI performing the exact function reserved for a human coach." On direct reading of `api/sensei-vision/proof/route.ts` (both the originally audited version and the reconciled branch's version), its gates (resistance visible, correction visible, correction held, rep quality, confidence) all grade whether the *submitted proof media* demonstrates the athlete executed an already-defined correction under resistance — closer to Vision's "what actually happened" evidence role than to approving a correction's content. It has no write access to any mission/authority table and cannot make anything become an active correction. This is recorded here rather than silently dropped, so the discrepancy between the initial read and the verified conclusion stays auditable.

## Directional comparison against external governance standards

DISCiPLIN is not certified against, affiliated with, or reviewed by any of the organizations below. The comparison here is limited to whether DISCiPLIN's *stated* design principles point in the same direction as publicly documented practice at these bodies — it is not a claim of equivalence, compliance, or endorsement.

- **IMMAF** (International Mixed Martial Arts Federation) has, per its own public materials, embedded safeguarding into its governance and lists data protection and privacy explicitly among athlete rights under its anti-doping framework, alongside a medical-data partnership (Safe MMA) that maintains athlete health records under independent clinical oversight. DISCiPLIN's Constitution places comparable weight on data ownership and human authority (coach over AI), but DISCiPLIN has no equivalent of Safe MMA's independent medical oversight, and this audit did not find a written data-protection/privacy policy specific to the `coach_relationships`/`mission_versions` data now in force — that should be authored before any real coach-athlete data flows through it. ([IMMAF governance](https://immaf.org/category/governance/), [IMMAF safeguarding/data rights coverage](https://immaf.org/2025/11/04/immaf-launches-this-is-sport-a-global-report-showcasing-the-growth-safety-and-impact-of-mma/))
- **Abu Dhabi Sports Council (ADSC)**, established 2006, runs a formal sports-data governance program with Statistics Centre – Abu Dhabi (SCAD) to standardize data collection, analysis, and methodology across the emirate's sports sector. DISCiPLIN's `coach_relationships`/`mission_versions`/`coach_audit_events` schema, with its audit-event table and immutable mission-versioning, is a materially stronger step toward the kind of structured, auditable data model this implies than the schema originally audited — but it remains a single-team schema, not one designed for federation-level reporting, aggregation, or oversight. That would be a distinct, larger design effort if UAE government integration is pursued. ([ADSC data governance award listing](https://mobile.stevieawards.com/mena/abu-dhabi-sports-council-uae-abu-dhabi-united-arab-emirates-abu-dhabi-sports-sector-data), [SCAD/ADSC agreement](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/government-news/statistics-centre-abu-dhabi-and-abu-dhabi-sports-council-sign-agreement-to-improve-quality-of-sports-life-in-abu-dhabi-ocwwkrvj))

**Conclusion:** DISCiPLIN's design intent (coach authority, evidence over marketing, data protection) is directionally consistent with how these bodies talk about governance and athlete data, and the reconciled implementation is a stronger realization of that intent than the schema originally audited. Nothing found in public sources suggests a specific technical bar DISCiPLIN now fails to clear — but nothing found constitutes a certification either. Any government-facing claim of "exceeding" these standards should be qualified as intent-alignment, not audited compliance, unless and until an actual review by those bodies takes place.
